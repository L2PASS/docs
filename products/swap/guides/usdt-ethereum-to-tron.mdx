You can bridge USDT from Ethereum (ERC-20) to Tron (TRC-20) with [L2Pass Swap](https://swap.l2pass.com/), a non-custodial interface that shows every route for the pair side by side. In test quotes on October 19, 2026, it offered three routes for this pair: L2Pass RFQ, the native USDT0 bridge and L2Pass Private, each with an estimated time of about 1.5 minutes. There is a 0% L2Pass fee on stablecoin swaps (up to 0.3% on private swaps), and every fee is already included in the amount you will receive, shown in the quote before you confirm.

## Routes for USDT from Ethereum to Tron

L2Pass RFQ and L2Pass Private both run on [NEAR Intents](https://near.org/intents), where solvers and market makers compete to fill your swap. Private executes it in a confidential environment.

| Route | Estimated time | Output | Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| L2Pass RFQ | About 1.5 minutes | Variable, with a minimum received | Pay to a one-time deposit address. |
| USDT native bridge (USDT0) | About 1.5 minutes | Fixed | Signed in your Ethereum wallet. Adds a LayerZero message fee paid in ETH. |
| L2Pass Private | About 1.5 minutes | Variable, with a minimum received | Deposit address. L2Pass fee up to 0.3%. |

No aggregator routes (LI.FI, LI.FI Intents, Relay, Aori or the OKX DEX aggregator) were offered for this pair in those test quotes. The quote lists any route that can fill the pair.

Test quotes for 1,000 USDT on October 19, 2026 showed these amounts received: 998.21 USDT on L2Pass RFQ, 999.7 USDT on USDT0 (plus a LayerZero message fee of about 0.005 ETH, about 11.92 USD) and 995.64 USDT on L2Pass Private. Because of the message fee, L2Pass listed L2Pass RFQ first. The live quote shows current numbers for your amount.

## How to bridge USDT from Ethereum to Tron

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open L2Pass Swap">
    Go to [swap.l2pass.com](https://swap.l2pass.com/), choose USDT on Ethereum as the source and USDT on Tron as the destination, then enter the amount and set the Tron address (it starts with T) that will receive the USDT.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Compare the routes">
    Each route card shows the expected output, minimum received, estimated time and fees. Default slippage is 0.05% (editable) and does not apply to fixed-output routes such as USDT0.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set a refund address">
    For L2Pass RFQ and L2Pass Private, set the refund address to an Ethereum wallet you control. If the swap cannot complete, your deposit is returned there.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pay by wallet or deposit address">
    On the USDT0 route, sign the transaction in your Ethereum wallet. On L2Pass RFQ and L2Pass Private, send USDT to the one-time deposit address from your connected wallet, any Ethereum wallet or an exchange, by address or QR code. If you send from an exchange, check that the amount that arrives matches the quote, since some exchanges deduct their withdrawal fee from it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Track the swap">
    For L2Pass RFQ and L2Pass Private, follow the status in **Recent swaps**: *Awaiting deposit*, *Deposit confirmed*, then *Complete*. A USDT0 transfer is a LayerZero message, so you can follow it on [LayerZero Scan](https://layerzeroscan.com/) with your Ethereum transaction hash.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Things to know

- **Gas on Ethereum only.** Sending ERC-20 USDT costs ETH for gas. The Tron network fee is deducted from the amount shown (1.71 USDT in test quotes on October 19, 2026), so you do not need TRX to receive. You need TRX later to move the USDT on Tron.
- **USDT0 message fee.** The USDT0 route charges a LayerZero message fee in ETH on top of gas. Compare it with the RFQ amounts before you choose.
- **Refunds.** On-chain routes revert (gas is not refundable). Deposit-address routes return your deposit to the refund address; provider and network fees may be deducted.
- **No partial fills.** A swap completes in full or does not execute.
- **Minimum amount.** Some routes have a minimum swap amount. If your amount is below it, the route is listed as unavailable with the minimum shown.
- **Compliance checks.** No account or KYC is needed for regular use, though a provider may request extra checks for a specific transaction. If a provider flags a swap as high risk under AML or sanctions rules, it decides whether to complete, refund or hold it, and a refund is not guaranteed.
